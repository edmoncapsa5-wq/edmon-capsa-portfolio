# Fictional creative-studio handoff — one extra request, one accountable decision

**Purpose:** A concrete example of how MarginLatch can be adapted to an agency that already uses Trello. This is **not** a real client case study, a live Trello integration, a connected board, or a claim that a buyer has adopted this process.

## Example situation

A studio has agreed to deliver a 60-second launch video and **two** consolidated revision rounds. After both rounds, a client requests a third round plus two additional 15-second versions. The studio does not yet know the true cost or whether the authorized client contact will approve it.

**Initial state:** `Needs scope decision`. **Never** label this request "Approved" merely because a team member said it sounds reasonable.

## Seven working stages

| Stage | Owner | Minimum evidence before moving forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. New client request | Account coordinator | Date, original wording, requested deliverable, relevant project and request ID |
| 2. Scope review | Project lead | Link/reference to the agreed deliverables and two included rounds; distinguish in-scope correction from genuinely new work |
| 3. Price and schedule impact | Authorized commercial lead | Estimated additional effort, proposed fixed fee or hourly rate, schedule impact, and options |
| 4. Awaiting client decision | Account lead | Client-facing quote/change note sent; note exact authorized approver; **do not start extra work** |
| 5. Approved for production | Project lead | Actual client decision and verifiable approval reference, plus accepted price and schedule |
| 6. Delivered / billing check | Delivery + finance owners | Delivered assets checked; record invoice reference only when an invoice genuinely exists |
| 7. Closed | Account/finance lead | Final disposition: included / billed extra / goodwill waiver / declined / deferred; no unresolved approval or billing question |

Suggested **Trello lists**: `Incoming`, `Scope decision`, `Quote required`, `Awaiting approval`, `Ready for production`, `Billing check`, `Closed`. This is a **suggested board structure for humans to configure**, not a feature of MarginLatch.

- Source: anonymized client request received 26 October
- Original scope: 60-second master / two rounds
- Proposed addition: two 15-second cutdowns and a third revision round
- Extra fee: **To be estimated by studio** (do not invent the client's rate)
- Delivery impact: **To be estimated**
- Decision: **Pending**
- Client approval evidence: **None yet**
- Invoice reference: **None yet**
- Next owner: Account manager; ask client to choose after receiving a real quote

### Friendly client response

> Happy to review those additional versions. The original agreement covers one 60-second master and two consolidated revision rounds, so we'll check the requested cutdowns and third round against that scope. We'll send a separate price and delivery-date impact for your approval before scheduling extra production. Alternatively, we can keep the original agreed scope and move the additional versions to a later phase.

## Three possible buyer decisions

1. **Approve as an extra:** record fee, timeline and actual authorized written acceptance; only then release the additional work and ultimately hand the decision to finance.
2. **Swap/defer:** confirm in writing what replaces what, or park extra work in a future phase. A swap is *not automatically free*.
3. **Goodwill waiver:** authorized studio owner elects to absorb a small request; record the decision and estimated effort. Do not count it as approved billable revenue.

## What a $99 MarginLatch setup would contribute

We would configure the Excel request/approval/invoice checks for **the studio's actual services**, make a branded change-approval document, write five studio-specific client replies, define a concise role-owner SOP, demonstrate one fictional example, and provide one first-use email assist plus one in-scope revision. We **would not** populate Trello cards, monitor clients, follow up each day, create integrations or act as an ongoing project coordinator.

**Fit check:** If the agency already handles all these decisions reliably in Trello/PM software, an additional workbook may add unnecessary duplication, and the $99 setup is not recommended.
