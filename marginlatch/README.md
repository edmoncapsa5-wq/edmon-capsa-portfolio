# MarginLatch — Client change requests, made billable

**A customized, no-subscription scope-control setup for small web, design and video studios.**

You quoted five pages. The client asks for a sixth. Someone agrees in chat, the team does the work, and later nobody can tell whether the addition was approved for billing.

MarginLatch is a lightweight process for checking the original scope, pricing new work, getting explicit approval, and checking that approved additions were invoiced. **It is a file-based pilot service, not a hosted SaaS platform.**

> **Founding setup: US$99, one time.** Proposed 24–48-hour delivery after cleared payment and complete intake, subject to a written confirmed order. This is not an instant checkout.

## The actual deliverables

| What you get | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Agency-specific extra-work tracker | Records agreed deliverables, revision limits, new requests, proposed fees and actual decisions |
| Branded change-request form | Summarizes what the client wants, added fee and timeline impact |
| Five editable client messages | Helps clarify scope, quote a new request, seek approval, defer work, and confirm next steps |
| One-page team instructions | Explains who logs a request, who approves pricing and what must happen before work starts |
| One revision round | Adjustments to the delivered templates within seven days |

The tracker can be supplied in Excel or as an offline browser-local file. There is no login or recurring subscription. It does **not** integrate with your agency systems, secure private client records on a server, send approval emails automatically, provide e-signatures, collect payments or substitute for legal review.

## Two-minute fit check

Before you spend $99, ask:

1. Where does your team check whether a new client request is inside the signed project scope?
2. Who records the extra price, scheduling impact, and client's explicit approval?
3. Who checks whether an approved extra was actually invoiced?

If your current project-management or invoicing software covers all three reliably, you probably do not need MarginLatch. If one part is consistently missed, the custom setup may help.
