# Operations Tracker — Portfolio Sample

## Scenario
A small remote team needs one simple tracker for recurring operational work without turning the sheet into a complicated project-management system.

## Objective
Make ownership, priority, due dates, status, blockers, and next actions visible at a glance.

## Sample fields
| Task ID | Workstream | Task | Owner | Priority | Due Date | Status | Blocker | Next Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OPS-001 | Client Onboarding | Verify intake documents | Admin VA | High | 2026-09-12 | In Progress | Missing signed form | Request signed form |
| OPS-002 | Reporting | Prepare weekly operations summary | Admin VA | Medium | 2026-09-13 | Ready | — | Send for review |
| OPS-003 | Billing Support | Match invoice to approval record | Accounting Support | High | 2026-09-11 | Held | Approval reference unclear | Escalate reference mismatch |
| OPS-004 | CRM | Review possible duplicate contacts | Admin VA | Medium | 2026-09-14 | Review | Conflicting addresses | Compare source records |
| OPS-005 | Documentation | Update onboarding SOP | Operations | Low | 2026-09-16 | Not Started | — | Review current process |

## Control logic
- **Ready** — work is complete enough for the next authorized step.
- **In Progress** — actively being worked.
- **Review** — evidence needs checking before action.
- **Held** — consequential ambiguity or missing authority prevents safe continuation.
- **Not Started** — accepted work not yet begun.

## Operating rule
Routine, reversible corrections should not create unnecessary client friction. Material ambiguity should be held and escalated with a proposed next action.

## What this demonstrates
- task tracking and prioritization
- status normalization
- blocker visibility
- exception handling
- concise operational reporting
- clear ownership and next-action discipline
